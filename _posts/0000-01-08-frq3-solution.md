## FRQ 3: Solution

**(a)** Direct substitution: $\frac{e^0 - 1 - 0}{0} = \frac{0}{0}$ ✓ Indeterminate

**(b)** First application of L'Hôpital's Rule:

$$\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{2e^{2x} - 2}{2x} = \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{2(e^{2x} - 1)}{2x}$$

Still $\frac{0}{0}$, apply again:

$$\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{4e^{2x}}{2} = \frac{4e^0}{2} = \frac{4}{2} = 2$$

**(c)** Two applications were needed. Each time we had $\frac{0}{0}$ form, justifying L'Hôpital's Rule.
